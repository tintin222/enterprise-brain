# Enterprise Brain

**AI employees for every department: hired by the managers who need them, working on their own in your mailboxes and systems, with people in charge.**

An AI employee is an agent with a job, a manager, duties, access and a probation level. A department manager hires one in the **Studio** by answering an analyst's questions; it then follows its mailbox or system by itself, keeps track of its open work for days if needed, and hands a person anything that needs one. Operations workers see what needs them on **Home** and handle it in one click; managers move each AI employee from *Shadow* to *Supervised* to *Trusted* as it proves itself; IT connects the systems and chooses what AI employees may do in them.

- **Two portals, one sign-in.** **Operations**, at the app's address, is the daily work. *Home*: "What do you need?", what needs me, what my AI employees did today. *Chat*: conversations with colleagues and AI employees together, where "@" names anyone or anything of the company. *Work*: every task, and everything that needs a person. *Mail*: the shared mailboxes AI employees follow, each email with what the AI employee did with it (managers and IT see all of them, everyone else their departments' ones). *Apps*: the apps, tables and calculations people work in. *Company*: departments with their people and AI employees, and what each AI employee did and how it is doing. The **Studio**, at `/studio`, is where things are designed. Its *Home*: "What do you want to build or change?", the Studio agent and the guided interview. *AI employees*: each one's job, duties, access, knowledge, probation and rules, coaching and versions. *Brain*: everything the company knows about itself, to browse and add to. *Apps*: making and changing apps, tables and calculations. *Ready-made*: 37 ready-made AI employees. *Settings*: connections, Teams and Chat, knowledge, people and roles, costs, the rules for building, audit log, installation. Everyone can open both. The switch in the header opens the same thing in the other portal (an AI employee's results and its design, a table used and changed), and old links keep working.
- **Work on their own.** Duties start by themselves when an email arrives, a set time comes, or a record appears in a connected system. Every piece of work is a task that can wait for a reply ("when the supplier answers, or in 3 days") and wake up again. Anyone can give an AI employee work in plain words, in the app or by forwarding an email.
- **People in charge.** On *Shadow* and *Supervised*, a person approves every change: every email sent and every write to a system. On *Trusted*, the manager sets the limits (amount, currency, changes a day, email domains). Workers can correct a change before approving it, and each correction, reasoned rejection or check marked wrong is kept as a coaching note. Each AI employee has a monthly budget.
- **People and AI employees in one conversation.** In *Chat*, several people and several AI employees work on one matter at the same time. Name an AI employee with "@" and it answers; name a process, a system, a table, a report, a document or a task and the AI employees can look it up, as far as *you* may see it. An AI employee takes part like a colleague: it answers when it is addressed, asks with a question card, and everything it does is a normal task (levels, approvals, costs and audit unchanged), so a Supervised one puts its email in the conversation as an approval card. Every task has a conversation where people comment and the AI employee reads the comments on its next step; the company brain is a participant anyone can ask.
- **Where people already work.** Each person chooses what reaches them outside the app: by default, approvals and questions an AI employee waits on arrive at once, everything else in a summary each morning. In **Microsoft Teams** and **Google Chat**, people give their AI employees work by message and get cards whose buttons approve, reject, answer or check; the card updates wherever the item was handled. By **email**, the buttons open a page where the person confirms.
- **Better over time.** A person marks a finished task as wrong and says why; the Studio turns the correction into a rule, replays recent tasks with it (nothing is sent or changed) and shows what would come out differently before the manager publishes the new version. *Company → Performance* measures AI employees against the targets (tasks finished alone, how long people take to help them, what is corrected later, what it costs), and each department's monthly budget stops its AI employees together at the limit.
- **Build what you need, in plain words.** One box on Home, *What do you need?*, takes anything: work for an AI employee now or every Monday, a question for the company's knowledge, a number worked out on the tables, or something to build. People describe **tables** ("supplier complaints: supplier, order number, problem, status, owner") and **apps** ("log a complaint, see the open ones by supplier, close them"), and say **calculations** as rules ("rank suppliers by complaints per 100 deliveries last month"); the platform makes them without anyone seeing a database, a deployment or code (tables live in its own database; apps are drawn from its own building blocks; calculations run in a QuickJS sandbox). Anything built is changed the same way ("rename Done to Closed, add a root cause"), shown in plain words and checked before it changes, with every version kept and a way back. IT decides who builds; personal data waits for the data protection officer, and sharing beyond a department for IT.
- **The company brain.** What the company knows about itself, in one place: its people, roles and what each of them knows; its processes (the steps, who does each one, in which system, the rules, the documents, what goes in and what comes out); its IT systems with their purpose, users, APIs and databases, where documents are kept, hosting and cloud; its data: the tables of its databases, read from the databases themselves through their connections, with what each table and column means in business words (written by people, suggested by Claude), which processes write and read them and where their data comes from, its other data sets (BI semantic models, Excel files, exports) and its BI reports with what each is for, who reads it, its measures, dimensions and screenshots; its clients, deals, customer issues, suppliers and products; its projects and tasks, who works on what now and what comes next; and its goals, decisions, know-how and company words. People fill it in by hand or just tell it what they know in their own words; sources bring the rest and keep it up to date (the app itself, and demo HR, IT inventory, intranet, CRM, ERP, service desk, project, data catalog, Power BI, Teams, Slack and email sources with made-up data until IT connects the real systems). Anyone can ask it ("Who knows the 8D process?", "What is happening with Petrokim?"); the CEO sees what needs attention, how projects stand and which know-how only one person has; AI employees and the Studio look things up in it, so new AI employees start from how the company really works.
- **The Studio.** The manager says what they need in their own words. With Claude, the Studio works like Claude Code for business: it looks at the mailboxes, folders, tables and systems, asks only what the manager alone can say, builds the AI employees, tables and screens, tries each AI employee on real examples and fixes what falls short, and asks IT for what isn't connected. Its AI employees work on each email, on a schedule, from a form, on each new file or record in a connected system, when another system calls them, or when people ask. Nothing is at work until the manager puts it to work. Without Claude, an analyst interviews the manager in rounds, each question with a recommended answer.
- **Connections.** Microsoft 365 and Gmail mailboxes; Outlook and Google calendars, where AI employees find times people are free and book meetings (the Interview Scheduler books interviews with a meeting link); SAP S/4HANA, SuccessFactors, Workday, Dynamics 365, Salesforce and HubSpot (preview); and **any web service or database** turned into named actions (with OAuth 2.0 or client certificates where the system asks for them), and **MCP servers**, whose tools become actions IT reviews, from an OpenAPI description, a few example calls or named SQL queries. **SFTP servers and shared folders** too: AI employees read and write files there, and each new file in a watched folder (a scanned invoice, a partner's order file) starts their duties. **Old systems without an API** are worked through their screens with Claude's browser use and computer use (a web system, or a desktop program in a remote desktop page): IT writes each action in plain words, the AI employee signs in with an account whose password it never sees, stays on the system's own pages, and can't change anything in an action that only reads. Built-in demo ERP, CRM, HR, ATS, IT and calendar systems let every AI employee practise from day one.
- **People and sign-in.** Microsoft 365 (Entra ID) and Google Workspace sign-in, or passwords. Permissions follow departments: people see their own departments' AI employees, tasks and data.
- **The platform underneath:** PostgreSQL with pgvector (embedded, zero-install locally), a knowledge base with hybrid search, document reading with OCR, Excel, a Node.js runtime powered by **Claude**, and an MCP server. One installation per company.

## Quick start

### One app, in one command

Requirements: **Docker** (Docker Desktop on Mac and Windows).

```bash
docker compose up -d
```

Open **http://localhost:3200**. The first start takes a few minutes: it builds Enterprise Brain and downloads PostgreSQL. You get the demo company **Acme Endüstri A.Ş.** with its departments, AI employees and ten demo people. The demo asks for one user name and password first (**admin** / **KahveKeyfi+5**, remembered by the browser for 30 days); then the sign-in page signs you in as any of the demo people with one click, so you can see what an HR manager, a recruiter, a quality manager or IT sees.

- **Claude:** put `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=…` in a `.env` file next to `docker-compose.yml` and run `docker compose up -d` again. Without it, everything works in a clearly labelled offline mode.
- **Your own company:** before the first start, set `EB_SEED_DEMO=false`, `EB_COMPANY_NAME` and `EB_MAIL_DOMAIN` in `.env`. The first person to open the app becomes its admin, and sets up Microsoft 365 or Google sign-in, connections and people in the Studio's *Settings*.
- **For colleagues:** the app listens on this computer only. Once your admin account exists, set `EB_BIND=0.0.0.0` and `EB_PUBLIC_URL` to the address they use (or put it behind your reverse proxy with HTTPS).
- **The demo's gate:** a demo installation on the internet is behind one shared user name and password. Change them with `EB_GATE_USER` and `EB_GATE_PASSWORD` in `.env`, or remove the gate with `EB_GATE=off`; on an installation without demo data, `EB_GATE_PASSWORD` puts the same gate in front of the sign-in page.
- **Coming from the two-app bundle** (Enterprise Brain with Paperclip)? Run `docker compose up -d --build --remove-orphans` once; your data stays.
- **Updating:** `git pull`, then `docker compose up -d --build`. Your data stays and the database is migrated on start. The demo company is made only on the very first start, so an older demo won't get newer demo people and departments; to start the demo afresh, `docker compose down -v` first (this deletes the data).

### With Paperclip (optional)

```bash
docker compose -f docker-compose.paperclip.yml up -d
```

Paperclip on http://localhost:3100 with your company, departments and AI employees in its org chart and the Enterprise Brain page in its sidebar, and Enterprise Brain on http://localhost:3200, without sign-in. See [docs/PAPERCLIP.md](docs/PAPERCLIP.md#the-bundle-paperclip-and-enterprise-brain-in-one-command).

### From source

Requirements: **Node.js 22.12+** and **pnpm 9**.

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm build          # builds the web console and the Paperclip plugin
pnpm start          # http://localhost:3200
```

On first start, Enterprise Brain creates the demo company **Acme Endüstri A.Ş.**:

- four departments (HR, Finance, Customer Service, IT) and the company-wide shared services, with their AI employees at work, and Operations, with no AI employees yet: its quality manager builds what they need (Phase 4)
- a knowledge base of company policies in English and Turkish
- sandbox mailboxes with job applications (PDF/DOCX CVs), supplier invoices generated from the sandbox ERP's purchase orders, customer emails and IT requests

**Sign-in:** people sign in with their own accounts. On the demo company the sign-in page lists ten demo people (an admin, department managers and workers), one click each, so you can see what each role sees; before it, a demo installation asks everyone for one shared user name and password (`admin` / `KahveKeyfi+5` by default; `EB_GATE_USER`, `EB_GATE_PASSWORD`, `EB_GATE=off`), which the browser remembers for 30 days and which the server checks on every request. A new installation without demo data asks the first person for the admin account; the admin then adds colleagues in **Studio → Settings → People and roles**, where Microsoft 365 (Entra ID) and Google Workspace sign-in are set up too. `EB_AUTH=open` turns sign-in off for local trials.

**Database:** nothing to install. Enterprise Brain runs PostgreSQL (with pgvector) *inside* the application, using [PGlite](https://pglite.dev), and keeps everything in the `.data/` folder: `db/` for the database, `files/` for uploads and `master.key` for encrypting connector secrets. Back it up by copying the folder while the server is stopped; move it with `EB_DATA_DIR`. For production, or when several servers share one database, use a PostgreSQL server (15 or later) instead:

```bash
# once, as a database superuser, in the target database:
CREATE EXTENSION vector;
# then point Enterprise Brain at it (tables are created and migrated on start):
DATABASE_URL=postgres://user:password@host:5432/enterprise_brain pnpm start
```

The Docker install runs PostgreSQL with pgvector next to the app (with Paperclip, one server holds both databases). Managed services (Azure Database for PostgreSQL, AWS RDS, Google Cloud SQL) support pgvector.

**Claude:** set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (default model Claude Opus 5.5, `claude-opus-5-5`). Without it, everything still works in a clearly labelled **offline mode** with deterministic fallbacks. See `.env.example` for all settings (Postgres, embeddings, API keys, Paperclip).

Development: `pnpm dev` runs the API with watch mode, and `pnpm dev:web` runs the console on http://localhost:5173 with the API proxied. Stakeholder answer links are built from `EB_PUBLIC_URL`, so during development set it to `http://localhost:5173`. Run the tests with `pnpm test` and the type checks with `pnpm typecheck`.

## Try it

1. **Hire in the Studio** (sign in as *Ayşe Yılmaz*, HR manager; *Studio* in the header): *Describe the job in an interview* (with Claude connected: *A guided interview*), paste the description above, answer the rounds (or *Use recommendation*), attach the demo CVs, and choose "Ask IT on my behalf" for the mailbox. Watch the job description build up on the right. Confirm: the CV Screener is hired on trial and tried on your CVs. Then *Put to work*.

Nobody starts anything: the CV Screener picks it up by itself. (If you hired a second CV Screener, pause the ready-made one on its page first, or both will screen it.)
3. **Home** (sign in as *Can Demir*, recruiter): the shortlist decision is waiting under *Needs you*. Approve it, or *Edit* a change before approving. *Work* shows the task's whole history.
4. **Company → an AI employee**: what it did this week, how it is doing, its latest tasks and its duties. *Talk to it*, or *Give work*: its talk opens in Chat with *Give as work* chosen, and what you write becomes a task for it. When the task is done, its answer comes back in the talk as a reply. *Open in the Studio* shows its design: its job, duties, what it may use, and *Probation and rules*, where its manager moves it to *Trusted* with limits or sets its monthly budget.
5. **Chat** (sign in as *Elif Arslan*, accounts payable): it works like Slack. You are in *#general* and *#finance* (with finance's AI employees); write "@Invoice Processor, Kaya Çelik says two September invoices are unpaid: check them and send the reminder" in *#finance*; type "@" to name a table of the brain, a process or a document too. It answers in the channel; on *Supervised* its email appears as an approval card for the finance manager, who decides it right there (or on *Home*, or by email), and it follows up. Hover a message to *React* or *Reply in thread*: the thread opens beside the channel, and its replies stay out of the channel. *New message* writes to a colleague (one direct message per set of people) or an AI employee; *Add a channel* makes a public, department or private one, *Browse channels* finds the public ones. Open any task from *Work*: its conversation is the page, comments wake the AI employee. Ask an AI employee something that is a colleague's job and it hands it over: "Handing this over to @Purchasing Assistant", and the colleague joins and answers. In the Studio's *Brain*, *Discuss* on a process opens its conversation with the company brain; asking it happens in Chat (*Company brain*, first among the direct messages), and a thing named in an answer opens in the Studio's brain.
6. **Coaching**: open a finished task in *Work* and say *It was wrong*, in plain words. Its manager finds the correction under the AI employee's *Coaching*, in the Studio: the Studio turns it into a rule, replays recent tasks with it (nothing is sent or changed) and shows what would come out differently; *Publish* makes it the next version.
7. **Company → Performance** (managers): what AI employees finish alone, how long people take to help them, what is corrected later and what it costs, against the targets, week by week. **Studio → Settings → Costs** sets each department's monthly budget, which stops all its AI employees at the limit.
8. **Studio → Settings → Connections** (IT): connect a web service, then *Actions*: import them from its OpenAPI description or a few example calls, name each in plain words, mark which change data, and try them.

10. **Approvals by email**: when an AI employee waits for an approval, the people who may decide it get an email with *Approve*, *Reject* and *Correct it first*. Without a mail connection they land in the demo outbox (*Mail → Sent*); their buttons open a page where you confirm. Your menu (top right) → *What reaches me* sets when and where.
11. **Teams and Google Chat** (IT): *Studio → Settings → Teams and Chat* walks through connecting your Azure Bot (and downloading the Teams app to upload) or your Google Chat app.
12. **Build what you need** (sign in as *Selin Acar*, quality manager): on the Studio's *Home*, say "I need a supplier complaints register: supplier, order number, problem, status (open, in progress, closed), received on", *Go*, *See the app*, *Make the app* (said on *Home* in Operations, it offers *Continue in the Studio*). 13. **Build with the Studio agent** (needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; sign in as *Selin Acar*, quality manager): *Studio → Describe it to the Studio agent*, for example "Supplier complaints come to quality@acme.com.tr. I want each one logged, the root cause confirmed by Kerem, the supplier asked for an 8D report and followed up until it's closed." 14. **The company brain** (sign in as *Mehmet Öz*, IT): open the Studio's *Brain*. ## How it fits with Paperclip (optional)

Paperclip is the control plane for AI-agent companies: org charts, goals, tasks, heartbeats, budgets and governance. By design it doesn't run agents or hold knowledge. Enterprise Brain doesn't need it, and can extend it **without forking** through four official extension points:

| | Integration | Result in Paperclip |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Company package** (`agentcompanies/v1`) | Departments → projects and lead agents; specialists → employees; scheduled processes → routines; plus the `enterprise-brain` skill |
| 2 | **`hermes_gateway` adapter** | Paperclip heartbeats run Enterprise Brain agents synchronously, with output, usage and cost |
| 3 | **Plugin** (`plugins/paperclip-plugin`) | Knowledge search, specialist agents and approvals as tools for every agent; an Enterprise Brain page and dashboard widget |
| 4 | **MCP server** (`/mcp`) | Governed access to SAP, Salesforce, sandbox systems and knowledge |

See [docs/PAPERCLIP.md](docs/PAPERCLIP.md).

## Status

Phase 1 is built: people and sign-in, AI employees with managers, probation levels, limits and budgets, long-running tasks, the work queue, mailbox and system watchers, named actions for web services and databases, the new app, and hiring in the Studio. Every capability is covered by automated tests, which run offline with a scripted LLM for the Claude paths; `apps/server/test/gate1.test.ts` runs the phase's gate end to end (a CV Screener hired in the Studio follows careers@ on its own at the Supervised level, and a recruiter handles its exceptions in the app). The Docker install (`docker compose up -d`) was built and run with Docker 29. Not yet proven in production:

- The **Claude-powered paths** (analyst interview, extraction, evaluation, chat, OCR) have been exercised with a scripted model that honours the same structured-output schemas, but not yet against the live API. Run the Studio scenario once with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set before a customer demo.
- Microsoft 365 and Google **sign-in** follow the providers' OpenID Connect documentation and are tested against a stand-in provider, not yet against live tenants.
- The enterprise connectors are **preview**: built from vendor API documentation and tested against recorded request/response contracts, but not against live tenants. Verify each one in the customer's environment.
- **Microsoft Teams** and **Google Chat** follow the Bot Framework's and the Chat API's documentation (signed calls, cards with buttons) and are tested against stand-ins for Microsoft and Google, not yet against live tenants.
- **SFTP** is tested against a real SSH server in the test suite (ssh2's), and **shared folders** against local folders; verify with the customer's SFTP servers and SMB or NFS mounts.

Phase 2 is built: approvals by email, notification preferences and the morning summary, Microsoft Teams and Google Chat, calendars, OAuth 2.0 and client certificates for connections, MCP servers, and SFTP servers and shared folders. `apps/server/test/gate2.test.ts` runs its gate end to end: a supplier's invoice arrives 12% over its purchase order; the Invoice Processor checks the 3-way match in the ERP, finds the exception by itself and asks the AP specialist in Microsoft Teams, saying why; she approves it on the card; it posts the invoice with her approval on record, and the finance manager's copy of the question (by email) now shows who decided. The card above is that approval as Teams draws it.

Phase 3 is built: **coaching with replay**, **performance reports** against the targets (tasks finished alone after probation, time for people to handle work in working hours, tasks corrected later, cost per task, time to hire), **cost reports and department budgets** (a department's AI employees stop together at its budget and their managers hear it), **Oracle databases** (named queries and changes, with no Oracle client software), and **old systems without an API worked through their screens**. With coaching, a person marks a finished task as wrong and says why; the Studio turns it into a rule, replays recent tasks with the new version as tests (reusing what the original tasks' replies, decisions and writes returned, so nothing is sent or changed) and shows what would come out differently; the manager publishes the new version or keeps the old one. Test runs never write to connected systems, even when a write was approved. `apps/server/test/gate3.test.ts` runs the phase's gate end to end: a correction is tested on past tasks, then goes live. Not yet proven:

- **Screens** run on a real headless Chromium against a real old-style web system and a canvas "desktop program" in the tests, with a scripted model; the browser use and computer use requests follow Claude's documentation and SDK types but have not run against the live API. Try an action with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set on one of the customer's systems before relying on it, and give the AI employee an account that can do no more than its actions need.
- **Oracle** is tested against a stand-in client (node-oracledb's thin driver is used as documented); verify with the customer's database.
- **SOAP web services** (from a WSDL) are left for later.

Phase 5 (AI employees first) has started: **the Studio is an agent** that builds a whole solution in one conversation (P5.1). `apps/server/test/studio.test.ts` runs it end to end with a scripted model: a quality manager describes how supplier complaints are handled; the Studio reads the mailbox, asks two questions, makes the table and the Complaint Handler, tries it on a real email, plans the app, and once it is put to work the next complaint is filed after the quality engineer's approval. `apps/server/test/studio-files.test.ts` does the same for work that comes as files: supplier invoices scanned into a shared folder are logged by an AI employee that picks up each new file. With `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set, try it on *Studio → Describe it to the Studio agent* before a customer demo: the conversation hasn't yet run against the live API.

Phase 4 is built: **tables**, **apps** and **calculations** people describe in plain words, **changes in plain words** to anything built (and to AI employees, through coaching), **one box** ("What do you need?") for any request, including **recurring work** ("every Monday at 9…"), an AI employee that **fills a table from email** hired with one answer, and **rules for building**: IT decides who builds, personal data waits for the data protection officer, sharing beyond a department waits for IT, everything keeps its versions with a way back, and IT sees everything built with its owner. Nothing built needs a deployment: tables live in the platform's database, apps are drawn from its building blocks, and calculations run in QuickJS compiled to WebAssembly (open source, inside the platform), with no network, files or host access and limits on time and memory. `apps/server/test/gate4.test.ts` runs the phase's gate end to end: a quality manager asks for a supplier complaints register in one sentence; the same day it is an app with its table, an AI employee files complaints from email into it (the quality engineer approves each record at first), and a monthly calculation ranks suppliers, with nobody seeing a database, a deployment or code. Not yet proven:

- The **Claude paths** of Phase 4 (reading a request in the box, proposing tables and apps, writing a calculation's code and writing it again when it fails on the real rows, changes, reading prose emails) follow the same structured-output schemas as before and are tested with a scripted model; without a key, requests and rules of the usual shapes are read from the words (in English and Turkish). Try the box, a table, an app and a calculation with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set before a customer demo.
- **Heavy data work** (Python on large files) isn't in the sandbox; a hosted open-source sandbox such as E2B or Daytona can take it later behind the same contract.

The **company brain** is built: what the company knows about itself in seven areas (organization, processes, IT and systems, data and reports, clients and partners, projects and work, strategy and know-how), entered by hand, told in plain words, or brought by sources that keep it up to date, and used by people (browse, ask, the CEO's overview, the map), by AI employees and by the Studio. `packages/brain/test/brain.test.ts` checks how what sources bring is merged (things found again by a source's id, an email or a name; links a source no longer has dropped; changes on the timeline; a person's values kept; what a person removed not brought back), search and the overview, and `apps/server/test/brain.test.ts` fills the brain from the demo sources and asks it: who knows and does a process, which systems a process uses, a database's tables, the news each new reading brings, what a manager changed surviving the next reading, a worker's note and know-how, the company brain's answers in Chat, an AI employee and the Studio looking things up, and the brain taught in Chat (a card of what it understood, kept by the person who taught it with their own rights) closing a gap in know-how. `apps/server/test/data-catalog.test.ts` checks the data catalog: tables with their columns in business words and the work that uses them, BI reports with their measures, dimensions and screenshots (served so they can't run as pages), lineage from a report back to the SAP tables, reading a demo database and a real one (PGlite through an SQL connection) without losing what people wrote, definitions suggested and kept, and the overview's data gaps; `packages/connectors/test/sql-schema.test.ts` reads PostgreSQL, MySQL, SQL Server and Oracle catalogs. Not yet proven:

- The **sources are demo integrations** with made-up data of the demo company. Real ones (SuccessFactors or Workday, ServiceNow, SharePoint, Salesforce, SAP S/4HANA, Jira, Microsoft Purview, the Power BI admin API, Microsoft Teams, Slack, Microsoft 365 mail) come next, each a source with the same contract (`packages/brain/src/sources/types.ts`): read what the system knows now, and the brain merges it.
- **Reading tables** is tested on PostgreSQL (PGlite) and on recorded catalog answers of MySQL, SQL Server and Oracle; try it on the customer's own databases (a read-only login is enough) before relying on it there.
- The **Claude paths** (answers, an AI employee's and the Studio's lookups, "Tell the brain", suggested definitions) are tested with a scripted model or offline. Try them with `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` set before a customer demo.

**Conversations** are built (`docs/CONVERSATIONS.md`, ADR 0007 and 0009): people and AI employees in one thread, in a new place, *Chat*, which works like Slack: channels (`#general` and one per department from the start, with the department's AI employees in it; public, department and private ones anyone makes, joined, left, renamed and archived), direct messages (one per set of people), threads under any message (each a conversation of its own, in a pane beside the channel, listed under *Threads* while followed) and emoji reactions, next to a task's conversation, a person's talk with an AI employee (the company brain is one, hidden; its talk is the first direct message) and the conversation about a thing in the brain; "@" names people, AI employees, things of the brain, tables, apps, calculations, files, documents and tasks, as far as the author may see them, and the AI employees get a card per mention to work from; each AI answer is a normal run, so a Supervised AI employee's email becomes an approval card in the conversation, decided there, on Home or by email; comments on a task wake it; mentions reach people by email. The Assistant, "Ask the brain" and "Talk to it" moved into Chat, and the older chats were adopted as conversations. Phase 2 put one composer everywhere: an AI employee hands a person's message over to the right colleague (once, never a chain); *Teach the brain* in Chat answers with a card of what the brain understood, kept by the person who taught it; *Give as work* turns a message into a task, in Chat and in the Home box, which is now the same composer with "@" and files (the Give work and Tell the brain dialogs are gone). AI employees built by the Studio agent count as Studio hires on *Performance*. `apps/server/test/conversations.test.ts` checks it end to end with a scripted model: who answers (the AI employees named, one AI employee answering another once and never a third time, the one that wrote last in a thread), the hand-over, visibility, unread and mention counts, the approval card and the follow-up, mentions kept plain when the author may not see the asset, the picker, the company brain's talk, teaching it and keeping the card, giving work from a channel (the answer in the thread under the message) and a talk, a task's conversation waking the task, the mention email (a thread's link too), the built-in channels and who is in them, public channels joined and left, renamed and archived, private ones hidden, one direct message per set of people, threads and their counts, reactions, the older chats and the topics of before channels. Not planned now: the Studio thread as a conversation, and retiring the guided interview.

**Two portals** are built (ADR 0008): **Operations** at the app's address for the daily work, and the **Studio** at `/studio` for designing AI employees, the company brain, apps and settings, with one sign-in and a switch in the header that opens the same thing in the other portal. The server and its permissions are unchanged. Old addresses (`/brain/…`, `/hire/…`, `/settings/…` and older ones) redirect, and old links in messages are rewritten as they are shown. `apps/web/test/paths.test.ts` checks the addresses (which portal a page is in, the links, the redirects, the twin pages), and the server tests check the links it builds (a thing in the Studio's brain, a document beside the search results, the building inventory, the connection sign-in).
