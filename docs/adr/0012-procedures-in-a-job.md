# ADR 0012: Procedures in a job

**Status:** accepted · 2026-10-13

## Context
A customer asked for "n8n-like features". People who think in flows expect decisions and loops, list and data steps, a little code, calls to other systems that answer later, handing work to a colleague, webhooks that answer the caller, forms anyone can fill in, retries, and a record of what each step got and gave. Until now a job was either a straight list of 14 typed steps (catalog templates, ADR 0002) or the autonomous tool loop (every AI employee the Studio built). The customer wants these features here, not an n8n next to the platform, and the product owner chose a flow view whose steps are changed in words and settings, not a drag-and-drop canvas.

## Decision
- **Logic is data, in the job.** `WorkflowStep` is recursive: a `branch` (cases with conditions, and `else`) and an `each` (a list or a count, batches, at most 200 items unless it says, what each round adds, what a failed round does) hold steps of their own. New steps: `transform` (set, filter, sort, aggregate, dedupe, pick, rename, limit; a pure function in `packages/core/src/transform.ts`), `code`, `call`, `chat.send`, `respond`, and `wait` for a callback. Expressions gain functions (dates, texts, lists), the company's `vars`, and a loop's `item` and `index`; a `when` takes the same expressions. A procedure is still an `AgentDefinition`: versioned, coached, checked like the catalog's jobs (`checkWorkflow`) and run under the probation levels, approvals, budgets and audit log like any job.
- **Code is a sandboxed escape hatch.** A `code` step runs in the QuickJS sandbox calculations use: no network, files, timers, modules or host functions, 5 seconds unless it says (30 at most) and 64 MB. Whatever reaches a system goes through connectors and their approvals, never through code.
- **Unique ids, keyed runs.** Step ids stay unique across the whole job, so `steps.<id>` works wherever a step sits. Where a step ran is its key (`price`, `lines#2/price` in the third round of `lines`): the run keeps the keys that are done, each loop's progress (so a run stopped for a person inside a loop goes on from that round), the attempts of a step, and its rows in the inspector.
- **`run_steps` is the inspector's store.** One row per attempt, with the input as the templates resolved and the output, each kept up to 64 kB with a flag when cut. Not `run_events` (a streamed timeline with 600-character previews) and not `runs.context` (rewritten on every save).
- **An error policy per step, a failure duty per job.** `retry` (1 to 5 more times, a fixed or doubling wait of at most 600 seconds), `timeoutSeconds`, and `onError`: `fail`, `continue` (the error is the result) or `skip` (leave out the steps after it). A job's `onFailure` tells a person instead of the manager and gives the failure to a colleague as work. A manager runs a finished run again from a top-level step: a new run of the job as it is now, with what the steps before it found.
- **Waiting for others goes through tasks.** A callback wait and a call to a colleague are task waits, like a wait for a reply: the task sleeps, wakes when the system posts to the run's address (a token whose hash `run_callbacks` keeps) or when the colleague's task ends (the `task.changed` event), and times out after the wait's days. Calls go at most three deep, never to the caller itself or to the company brain.
- **One way in per AI employee.** Each AI employee has its own webhook address (an unguessable one, or a readable one with its secret, or a signature of the body) and its own public form link (`agent_endpoints`), for the duties it declares. A `respond` step shapes the answer a waiting caller gets. The inbound-webhook connector, whose event nothing served, is retired; the runs route with the API key stays for machines.
- **The Studio writes procedures in words.** When a person describes fixed steps, Claude writes them in plain kinds (`read_file`, `if`, `for_each`, `ask_colleague`, `post_in_chat`, `answer_caller`…), which `compileProcedure` turns into the job's steps and `checkWorkflow` checks; problems go back to Claude, and the person never sees the JSON.
- **A flow view, not a canvas.** The job is drawn from its definition by a pure layout (`layoutFlow`): what starts it, its steps, a decision's cases side by side, a loop framed. A step's settings change in a form, checked before a new version is saved; the shape of the job changes in words.

## Consequences
- A job can be anything from a fixed procedure to an AI employee working each task out, and mix them: an `agent` step inside a loop, a procedure that asks a colleague who works things out.
- Every run is inspectable step by step, and a failure can be fixed and the work picked up again from the step that failed.
- Other systems work with an AI employee directly, with its own credential, and can get its answer in the same call; the company's people and its customers reach it through a form.
- The recursive schema needs hand-written TypeScript types next to a lazy Zod union, and runs saved before it (no frames, no attempts) are read as they were.
- A timeout cannot stop a call to a system already under way, which may still finish: retries suit steps that can safely run twice.
- Waiting callers and the limits on calls are counted per instance, which fits the single process the platform runs in today; workers on a queue are a later wave.
- Approvals inside a loop can fill the work queue: a loop's `maxItems` caps it.
- Not in this wave: a drag-and-drop canvas, an n8n bridge, exporting and importing AI employees, new app connectors (Slack, Google Sheets, Jira), running again from inside a loop's round, and a chat widget for other sites.
