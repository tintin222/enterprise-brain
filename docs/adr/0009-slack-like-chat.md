# ADR 0009: Chat like Slack: channels, direct messages, threads and reactions on the one conversation

**Status:** accepted · 2026-10-09

## Context
Chat was one flat list of conversations (free topics, talks with an AI employee, a task's and a thing's conversation), each a single thread of messages with a quote-style reply. The product owner asked for the messaging part to work like Slack: channels people are in, direct messages, threads that open beside the conversation, and emoji reactions, with AI employees taking part as colleagues as they already did. ADR 0007 had made every talking thing one conversation primitive; the question was how to add Slack's shapes without a second one.

## Decision
- **The same primitive, three more kinds.** A channel, a direct message and a thread are conversations (`kind: channel | dm | thread`); the free `topic` is retired. A channel has a `name` (`#finance`), unique in the company for good; a direct message has a `dm_key` (its people, sorted), so the same people always find the same one; a thread has a `parent_id` and is *about* its root message (`about_id`), one per message. Permissions, read marks, cards, the live stream, the turn planner and the mention emails work on all of them unchanged.
- **Threads are conversations, not a flag on messages.** The thread pane is the ordinary conversation view of the thread's conversation; an AI employee's turn in a thread is a run like any other (`triggerRef` the thread), its approvals are cards in the thread, and read marks per thread give the *Threads* list and its unread counts for free. The parent hears an `updated` event for the root message when a reply comes, so the channel shows "3 replies" at once; a thread's replies never count as unread for the channel. Threads are offered under channels, direct messages and talks, not under a task's or a thing's conversation.
- **Built-in channels follow the company.** Every company has `#general` (everyone) and one channel per department, with the department's people and its AI employees at work in it; the app makes them at start and keeps them in step with the departments (names, who may read them, their AI employees), and joins each person to theirs when their list is asked for (reading from now, once in five minutes per person). A department channel is read by its department and IT, like the department's tasks and mail; an open department (shared services) gets a public channel. Built-in channels cannot be archived, renamed or left; nobody leaves `#general` or their own department's channel.
- **Direct messages are people's.** Their people are fixed: a colleague named with "@" does not join, nobody is brought in or leaves; a direct message has no owner. A person's talk with an AI employee is its direct message and is listed with the others; one AI employee alone in "New message" opens that talk, and the company brain's is always first.
- **Who answers.** In a channel or a direct message between people, an AI employee answers only when named with "@" (it reads the rest on its next turn); in its talk, always; in a thread, when named, else the AI employee that wrote last in the thread (so "Thanks, send it today" under its answer reaches it), else the one whose message the thread is under, else the talk's. Work given in a channel or a direct message is answered **in the thread under the message that gave it**, so the channel stays readable; in a talk or a thread the answer comes right there, as before.
- **Reactions are a table, not messages.** `conversation_reactions` holds one row per person, message and emoji, from a fixed list of sixteen; a change publishes `updated` for the message, to the conversation and to the thread under it. Nobody answers a reaction.
- **Adoption at start, in code.** The free topics of before become direct messages when they were two people's private talk (unless those two already have one: then a private channel), else channels named from their titles (`kaya-çelik-invoices`, de-duplicated with `-2`, `-3`…); it runs with the other start-up adoptions and is idempotent. The migration changes the schema only.
- **Naming.** "Channel" already named Teams and Google Chat in the code (`/api/channels/`, `channel_accounts`, `ChatChannelId`): everything here stays under *conversations* in code and routes; "channel" is a kind, a word in the UI and in the docs.

## Consequences
- One place, Chat, now reads like Slack: channels, direct messages and threads on the left, the conversation in the middle, the thread beside it; one pane at a time on a phone. The task page, the AI employee's page and the brain's *Discuss* keep embedding their conversations as before.
- A channel can be large: lists carry its first eight members and a count, the brief names at most forty participants, and the sidebar polls every twenty seconds rather than streaming the whole company.
- A thread's own address (`/chat/<thread id>`) redirects to its channel with the thread pane open; mention emails about a thread link there directly.
- Not in this version: pins and channel topics, search across messages, editing and deleting messages, presence, "follow thread" on its own, mirrored Teams and Google Chat groups.
