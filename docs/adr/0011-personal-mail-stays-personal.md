# ADR 0011: A person's mail stays personal

**Status:** accepted · 2026-10-13

## Context
The product owner wants the platform to read their Gmail and turn the emails that need them into tasks. The platform already reads mail, but company-wide: a mailbox is a connection (`connector_instances`, set up by admins), its messages are polled into the shared mail pipeline (`mail_messages`), shown on the Mail page to managers and IT, read by the brain's email source and handled by AI employees. A person's inbox must not become company data by being connected the same way.

## Decision
- **A person's mailbox is a `mail_accounts` row, not a connection.** One per person and provider, holding the address, the granted scope, the connector's cursor, the refresh token encrypted with the platform's master key, whether tasks are on, when it was last checked and what went wrong. It is never a `connector_instances` row: nothing of it appears on the Mail page or under Connections, no AI employee can read it, the brain's sources never see it, and `mail_messages` stays untouched.
- **Consent with the sign-in client.** Connecting goes through Google's consent screen with the Google OAuth client the installation already uses for sign-in (`openid email https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly`, offline access, consent forced so a refresh token comes), behind its own sealed cookie, from `/api/me/mail/gmail/start` to `/api/me/mail/gmail/callback`. The ID token is checked as for sign-in; the granted scope must include `gmail.readonly` and a refresh token must be present, else nothing is connected and the person is told why. Any Google account may be connected: the consent proves the person controls the mailbox, and the to-dos it makes are theirs alone. Without a Google client the dialog says what IT must set up (Google sign-in, the Gmail API, the scope, the redirect URI, which `/api/info` names).
- **Read only, and only what a task needs.** The account is polled through the existing `gmail` connector with a per-person context, up to 25 new messages a check; the first check only sets the starting point, so older mail is never read. For each message that needs the person something is kept as a to-do: a title, a few lines of detail, a due date when the mail names one, the sender and subject, and a link back to the message in Gmail. The body is never stored. The reader is the model when available (one structured call per batch, the first 4,000 characters of each message, the person's open projects so a to-do can land in one), otherwise a plain rule: "Reply to <name>: <subject>". Automatic senders, promotions and social mail, and senders the person said *Not a task* about are never sent to the model and never become tasks.
- **Paced by the watcher.** The watcher's pass checks the due accounts of a company (tasks on, not checked in five minutes) after the shared mailboxes; errors go on the account (`status: error`, `last_error`) and into the pass's result, never into the work queue. A grant Google no longer accepts is told as "Connect Gmail again"; *Check now* answers 409 then.
- **The person is in charge.** *Tasks from mail* can be switched off (the account stays), *Not a task* on a mail to-do quiets its sender, and *Disconnect* revokes the token at Google (best effort) and deletes the row; the to-dos already made stay, as the person's.

## Consequences
- A worker connects their own mail without asking IT for anything beyond the Google client IT already set up for sign-in; IT only registers a second redirect URI on it.
- The shared mail pipeline, the Mail page, the connections and the brain keep their one meaning: the company's mailboxes. A person's inbox never shows up there.
- The platform never sends mail or drafts replies for a person, and reads nothing but the inbox; Outlook and Microsoft 365 accounts can follow the same shape (a `mail_accounts` row, the installation's Microsoft client) later.
